Oak or walnut cutting board?

Start with your kitchen and how you use a board, not a ranking of wood species. Both oak and walnut can be good everyday companions.

Oak: structure and resistance to visible marks

Oak has a visible grain and often creates a lighter impression. In our experience it is more resistant to visible use marks than walnut. It still needs cleaning, drying and occasional surface care.

Read more

The actual piece of timber matters: colour, growth rings, knots and construction all change the result. Do not assume every oak board will look or feel the same.

Walnut: warm colour and a different feel

Walnut's darker heartwood can create contrast in a light kitchen or a quieter transition alongside dark cabinets. It is softer than oak and can show use sooner.

Read more

Hardness alone is not a complete guide to knife friendliness or daily comfort. Consider your knives, preferred feel, construction and care, as well as the board's weight.

Compare the boards, not just the woods

Two boards of different sizes or constructions are not a like-for-like comparison. Check dimensions, thickness, weight and how easily you can lift, wash and store each one.

Read more

For serving, the presentation and handling may lead the choice. For daily preparation, the clear working area can matter more. We can help you compare available pieces and their grain before you decide.

Questions before you choose

Which wood suits a light kitchen?

Walnut can add contrast, while oak can continue a lighter palette. The actual worktop and grain matter.

Can oak develop dark marks?

Yes. Tannins can react with iron and moisture. A dark mark is not automatically mould; ask if you are unsure.

Is walnut always more expensive?

Compare actual boards. Size, construction, wood selection and details all influence price.
