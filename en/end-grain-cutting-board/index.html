What end grain means

Pieces are arranged so that the ends of their fibres face upwards. The arrangement creates the visible pattern and the character of the working surface.

Read more

End grain is not automatically better than edge grain. Workmanship, suitable timber and the way the board is used are equally important. Choose for your cooking routine rather than a pattern alone.

Substance has weight

A large end-grain board can be substantial and heavy. That may suit a permanent preparation area, but makes lifting, cleaning and putting it away a different task.

Read more

Before choosing, measure the working area and consider how you will carry and wash the board. A lighter or smaller format may be more practical if it needs to move often.

Look at joints and the whole surface

Check the finish, edges, flatness and joints. A crack or opening joint is different from an ordinary knife mark and should be assessed rather than covered with more oil.

Read more

Wood responds to changing moisture. Cleaning and allowing both sides to dry are important. Suitable care supports the surface, but does not repair structural damage.

Questions before you choose

Is end grain always better?

No. Compare workmanship, dimensions, handling and intended use as well as construction.

Can an end-grain board crack?

Wood can move and develop damage, especially with unsuitable moisture exposure. Ask for an assessment of cracks or opening joints.

How should I care for it?

Hand wash briefly, wipe dry and let both sides dry freely. Follow the care guide and apply suitable care thinly.
