Workshop buying checklist

How can you recognise a quality cutting board?

A quality cutting board is not defined by a “premium” label. Look for clearly identified material, suitable construction and thickness, an even surface, clean workmanship and repairability. The board should fit its intended use and its production should be understandable.

Six checks

What should be clear before you buy?

1. Material

The wood species and construction should be named. Vague phrases such as “real-wood look” are not enough.

2. Construction

Long grain, end grain or glued segments should be cleanly built and appropriate for the intended use.

3. Thickness

Thickness should suit the format. More material adds refinishing reserve, but also weight.

4. Surface

It should feel even and pleasant rather than sticky. The finish and care requirements should be stated.

5. Stability & edges

The board should sit flat. Cleanly rounded edges make heavier boards easier to handle.

6. Repairability

Solid wood can often be cared for and refinished when enough material remains. This does not replace sound construction.

From our workshop

Quality starts before sanding.

A smooth surface cannot permanently hide material defects. Before machining, we examine grain direction, cracks, tension, possible dimensions and the planned construction. Only then do cutting, gluing, shaping and finishing follow.

A board does not need to be as thick or heavy as possible. Good construction means it can actually be used, cleaned and moved safely.

Warning signs

What weakens a quality claim?

Unclear wood species

Without material information, properties and care are difficult to assess.

Contradictory dimensions

Missing thickness or changing measurements make planning harder.

No care guidance

Wood needs cleaning, drying and occasional maintenance.

Sticky surface

Excess or unsuitable finish can make use unpleasant.

Aggressive superlatives

“The best board” does not replace material and construction details.

Unclear production

Origin wording should explain real work rather than merely claim trust.

Understand price without confusing it with quality.

Large formats, end-grain construction, material thickness, selection and multiple workshop stages can explain a higher price. Price alone does not prove suitability or workmanship.

FAQ

Questions before buying

How can you recognise a quality cutting board?

Look for clearly identified material, suitable construction and thickness, an even surface, clean edges and realistic care or refinishing guidance.

Does a good cutting board need to be very thick?

No. Thickness should suit size and use. A board that stays on the worktop can be substantial; a frequently moved board should remain manageable.

Is end grain automatically better than long grain?

No. Both can be well made. End grain is more complex and usually heavier, while long grain can be more practical for a movable board.

Does a high price prove quality?

No. Price can reflect material and labour, but does not replace clear details about construction, dimensions, finish and care.

Can a quality cutting board be refinished?

Solid wood with enough material can often be sanded and treated again. Open joints, deep cracks or severe warping need assessment first.
