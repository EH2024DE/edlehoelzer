End Grain vs. Edge Grain: Which Board Fits You?

Both constructions have their place. End grain is more characterful and kinder to knives. Edge grain is lighter, simpler and very practical for daily use.

Send inquiry View all products

The blade meets the fibres differently

With end grain, the wood fibres face upward. With edge grain, they run along the surface. That difference changes weight, feel, appearance and how much effort goes into the board.

Neither construction is automatically right for everyone. The best choice depends on how the board will be used.

End grain: more presence, more care

End grain is interesting for intensive cutting, good knives and a board that may stay visible. It is heavier, more complex to make and asks for regular care.

Edge grain: practical and direct

Edge grain can be lighter, calmer and easier to handle. For everyday kitchens where the board is moved often, it may be the better fit.

Decide by the moment, not the label

If you cut intensively and want a substantial work surface, end grain may make sense. If you want a reliable board for daily movement and cleaning, edge grain may be more practical.

FAQ

Common questions

Can the board be engraved?

Yes, if the wood, size and placement make sense. Short names, dates or initials usually work best.

How do I care for a solid wood board?

Wipe it clean, let it dry freely and refresh the surface with a thin layer of suitable oil or balm when it looks dry.

Is end grain better for knives?

End grain is especially kind to knives because the blade moves between upright fibres instead of cutting across them.

Can I request a custom size?

Yes. Send a short inquiry with size, use case and wood preference, and we will check what is sensible.
