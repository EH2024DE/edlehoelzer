Choose more than a colour

Grain, surface, weight and size all influence how a walnut board feels in everyday use. A compact board that is easy to lift serves a different purpose from a heavy board that stays on the worktop.

Read more

For our pure walnut boards, we select the darker heartwood. Natural differences in colour and grain remain part of the material. A photograph represents an individual piece, not a uniform industrial colour.

Working board or serving board?

Both are possible, but the useful format may differ. Cutting needs enough clear working area; serving also depends on what you will carry, arrange and place on the table.

Read more

Think about where the board will live and whether you need to move it often. A dark walnut surface creates contrast on a light worktop and can sit quietly alongside darker cabinetry. Judge the whole piece, not only the wood species.

Walnut or oak?

In our experience, oak is more resistant to visible use marks than walnut. Walnut is softer, so its surface can show use sooner. Both can be good everyday boards.

Read more

Do not decide on hardness alone. Your knives, preferred feel, kitchen design, the board's weight and your care routine also matter. We help you compare actual pieces rather than treating a hardness number as a complete buying guide.

End grain or edge grain?

Construction changes the visible grain and the character of the working surface. Compare the available formats and their weight as well as their appearance.

Read more

A larger end-grain block may suit a permanent preparation area. A smaller edge-grain board can be easier to lift and put away. Neither description alone tells you whether a board fits your kitchen.

Care with a light hand

Clean briefly by hand, dry with a cloth and let both sides release moisture. Apply suitable care thinly and remove excess rather than leaving a glossy layer.

Read more

Wood moves as moisture changes. Avoid prolonged soaking, dishwashers and drying against strong heat. If a board needs more than routine care, ask us whether refurbishment is appropriate for its condition.

Questions before you choose

Is walnut suitable for daily cutting?

Yes, when construction, dimensions and care suit the intended use. Expect natural signs of use rather than an untouched decorative surface.

Is walnut harder than oak?

No. In our workshop experience, oak is more resistant to visible marks. The choice should also consider feel, weight and appearance.

Why do walnut boards look different?

Natural colour and grain vary between pieces. Wood selection and construction also affect the finished appearance.
