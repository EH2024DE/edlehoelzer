Size

Small kitchen or fixed worktop?

A compact board is easier to move and store. A large board gives more working surface, but needs space and ideally stays out.

Weight

Easy to move or firmly in place?

More weight gives stability and presence. Less weight is more practical if the board is often moved, cleaned or stored away.

Use

Cutting, BBQ or serving?

For daily cutting, practicality matters. For BBQ and heavier work, the board may be more substantial. For serving, look and feel become more important.

Care

A wooden board stays good when it is treated well.

Solid wood does not belong in the dishwasher. Cleaning, drying and occasional oiling decide how well the surface ages over time.

FAQ

A short orientation before buying.

Which cutting board fits a small kitchen?

Compact or medium-sized boards work well in small kitchens because they are easier to move and store. Very heavy end-grain boards need more fixed worktop space.

When does a heavy end-grain board make sense?

A heavy end-grain board mainly suits large worktops, BBQ, heavier cutting and users who want to leave the board out permanently.
