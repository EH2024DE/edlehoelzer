Which wood types work for cutting boards?

Well-prepared hardwoods that create a durable, maintainable surface can work for cutting boards. Oak, walnut, beech, maple and ash are all possible choices. Timber quality, drying, construction and intended use matter more than a simple species ranking.

Before comparing

Species alone does not decide quality.

Two boards made from the same species can feel very different because of grain direction, sorting, thickness and construction.

Hardness

Affects surface resistance, but says little about construction or workmanship on its own.

Pores

Shape texture and feel. The final sanding determines how even the surface feels.

Weight

Depends heavily on size, thickness and construction. More weight gives stability but makes handling harder.

Colour & patina

Light, use and care change natural timber. Product photographs therefore remain a guide.

Common choices

Useful differences without declaring a winner.

Oak

Light to medium brown, strongly structured and noticeably heavy in larger formats. Tannins can react with iron and moisture.

Walnut

Dark, warm colour with natural variation. A strong option when the board will also be visible while serving.

Beech

Lighter and often more restrained. Construction and care matter more than assuming beech is automatically simple.

Maple

A light, often quiet-looking surface. The exact species, sorting and finish should be clear.

Ash

Pronounced grain with lively light-and-dark movement. Individual boards can vary considerably.

Cherry

A warm colour that can change visibly under light. Patina is part of its material character.

“Acacia” is not always precise.

The trade name may refer to different woods. Without a clear material description, properties and origin are difficult to assess.

Bamboo is not wood.

Bamboo is a grass. Boards are normally built from many glued strips and should be assessed differently from solid timber.

FAQ

Common questions about cutting-board woods

What is the best wood for a cutting board?

There is no single best species. Oak, walnut, beech, maple and ash can all work when drying, construction, surface and format suit the use.

Is oak suitable for cutting boards?

Oak can be suitable. It has a pronounced grain and can be relatively heavy. Its tannins may react with iron and moisture.

How does walnut differ from oak?

Walnut usually appears darker and calmer, while oak is lighter and more strongly structured. Construction, thickness and weight remain important.

Is bamboo a type of wood?

No. Bamboo is a grass and bamboo boards are normally made from many glued strips or segments.

Does hardness decide quality?

No. Hardness is only one factor alongside timber quality, drying, construction, surface, size and use.
