Oak in everyday use

In our workshop experience, oak is more resistant to visible use marks than walnut. It can be a good choice if durability of the surface matters to you. Colour, grain and weight should still be part of the decision.

Read more

The open-looking grain is part of the wood's character. Judge the finished surface and workmanship rather than the appearance of pores alone. Natural colour changes and patina can develop with use.

Edge grain or end grain?

Edge grain shows the lengthwise grain; end grain shows the ends of the fibres. Both can make useful boards. Compare the actual dimensions, thickness and handling rather than choosing by the construction name alone.

Read more

A board for breakfast or serving may need to be easy to carry. A large preparation or BBQ board may remain on the worktop. Choose a juice groove where it suits the food and board, not simply as decoration.

Surface changes need context

Dark marks on oak are not automatically mould. Oak's tannins can react with iron and moisture. If you are unsure about a mark, ask us before deciding how to treat it.

Read more

Do not treat every dark patch as the same issue. Condition, smell, moisture exposure and the actual surface need to be assessed. Keep the board clean and let it dry freely instead of leaving it damp against the worktop.

Made to keep using

Suitable oak boards can be refreshed or refurbished when their condition allows it. Care and careful drying support long-term use, but do not make wood immune to damage.

Questions before you choose

Is oak suitable for cutting boards?

Yes, with suitable construction, finishing and care. Choose a particular board that fits your use.

Is oak better than walnut?

Neither is universally better. Oak is more resistant to visible marks in our experience; feel, appearance and weight also matter.

Can an oak board be refurbished?

Often, where the substance and condition are suitable. We assess it before recommending a service.
