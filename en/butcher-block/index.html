For the moment after the real work starts

A butcher block is not just a larger cutting board. It is a fixed working surface for meat, bread, BBQ prep and everything that benefits from weight under the knife.

That weight is part of the experience. A substantial block moves less quickly, gives larger food more room and makes cutting feel calmer.

Why material and thickness matter

A useful butcher block needs enough substance for stability, rounded edges for handling and a surface that can be cleaned, dried and refreshed.

End grain can make sense for intensive work. Edge grain can be right when a calmer look or a specific format matters more.

When a butcher block is not the right choice

If the board has to be stored after every use, a smaller cutting board may be more practical. A butcher block works best when it has a place where it can stay.

This is not a board for the drawer. It is a piece of working surface.

FAQ

Common questions

Can the board be engraved?

Yes, if the wood, size and placement make sense. Short names, dates or initials usually work best.

How do I care for a solid wood board?

Wipe it clean, let it dry freely and refresh the surface with a thin layer of suitable oil or balm when it looks dry.

Is end grain better for knives?

End grain is especially kind to knives because the blade moves between upright fibres instead of cutting across them.

Can I request a custom size?

Yes. Send a short inquiry with size, use case and wood preference, and we will check what is sensible.
